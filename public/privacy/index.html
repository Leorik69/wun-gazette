3. Personal data we collect

3.1 Data generated automatically when you visit the website

When your browser requests a page from this website, our hosting infrastructure automatically receives and records technical information about the request. This is ordinary operation of web servers and content delivery networks, and it typically includes:

  • your IP address;
  • technical identifiers of your device and browser, such as the user-agent string, browser and operating-system version, device type, and preferred language;
  • the date and time of the request and the time zone;
  • the page or file requested, the amount of data transferred, and the response status;
  • the address of the referring page, where your browser sends it;
  • security and diagnostic data generated by the hosting platform, such as error codes and request identifiers used to detect and block attacks and abuse.

We use this information in aggregate to keep the website available, fast and secure, and to understand at a general level which content is read. We do not use it to build personal profiles, to track you across other websites, or to make decisions about you.

3.2 Data you give us directly

This website contains no account registration, no contact form, no newsletter subscription and no payment function. The only way to send us personal data through the website is to contact us voluntarily, usually by email.

If you write to us, we receive and process:

  • your email address, your name or the name you sign with, and any other contact details you include;
  • the content of your message, including any personal data you decide to mention in it;
  • the technical metadata of the email itself (for example, the date and time of sending and the message routing information).

You choose what to send us. Please share only what is necessary for your question. We do not ask for, and do not need, special categories of personal data (for example data about health, political opinions, religion, ethnic origin, biometric data or trade-union membership) or data about criminal convictions. If you send such data to us voluntarily, we will process it only to handle your message, and we may delete it after that.

3.3 Data we do not collect

Through this website we do not knowingly collect: account credentials, payment or financial data, government-issued identity numbers or copies of identity documents, precise location data, biometric data, or data of children. Any such data is processed, if at all, on the separate platforms described in section 2, under their own terms and notices.

4. Purposes and legal bases of processing

We process personal data only when we have a valid legal basis, in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (“GDPR”) where it applies, and the Law of Georgia on Personal Data Protection. Our purposes and legal bases are the following.

  • Operating, maintaining and securing the website — serving pages and files, keeping the service available and reasonably fast, monitoring errors, protecting the website against attacks, abuse, spam and unauthorised access, and keeping the necessary audit and diagnostic records. Legal basis: our legitimate interests in operating and protecting our service and in ensuring the security of our systems and of visitors (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f); equivalent ground under Georgian law).
  • Understanding how the website is used in aggregate — measuring which pages are viewed and diagnosing technical problems, using technical logs rather than tracking technologies. Legal basis: our legitimate interests in improving and correctly operating the website, without overriding your rights and freedoms (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f); equivalent ground under Georgian law).
  • Answering your enquiries and requests — reading your message, replying to you, and keeping a record of the correspondence and of any request you make about your rights. Legal basis: our legitimate interests in communicating with people who contact us and in handling their requests (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)); where you contact us in order to enter into a relationship with us, processing that is necessary to take steps at your request before entering into a contract (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)); and, where required, your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)).
  • Exercising and defending legal rights, and complying with the law — establishing, exercising or defending legal claims, responding to lawful requests from courts, supervisory authorities and other public bodies, and meeting legal, accounting and record-keeping obligations that apply to us. Legal basis: compliance with a legal obligation to which we are subject (GDPR Art. 6(1)(c)) and our legitimate interests in establishing, exercising or defending legal claims (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)); equivalent grounds under Georgian law.

Where processing is based on our legitimate interests, we have assessed those interests against your rights and freedoms and consider that they do not override them, because the data involved is limited to what is strictly necessary for running, protecting and improving a public website, and because we do not use it to make decisions about you or to track you individually. You have the right to object to this processing at any time — see section 12.

Where processing is based on your consent, you may withdraw that consent at any time. The withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before the withdrawal. Consent is not a condition for using this website.

We do not carry out automated decision-making, and we do not use personal data collected through this website for direct marketing, for selling goods or services to you, or for scientific or statistical research purposes under Art. 89 GDPR.

5. Cookies and similar technologies

Cookies are small text files that a website asks your browser to store. We do not use them on this website.

  • We do not set advertising, marketing, analytics or profiling cookies, and we do not use tracking pixels, web beacons, fingerprinting or similar technologies.
  • We do not allow third-party advertising or analytics providers to place cookies or other identifiers through this website.
  • Because we do not use non-essential cookies or comparable identifiers, we do not display a cookie consent banner. If we introduce non-essential cookies or analytics in the future, we will update this Policy and ask for your consent where the law requires it before those technologies are used.

Your browser may still store technical data that we do not control and cannot read, such as its own cache or your browsing history, and your browser or extensions may apply their own settings. Most browsers let you block or delete stored data and manage cookies for all websites. Doing so will not prevent you from using this website.

We do not respond to “Do Not Track” browser signals with a separate technical mechanism, because we do not track visitors across websites or over time in the first place.

8. How long we keep personal data

We keep personal data only for as long as is necessary for the purposes described in this Policy, and then delete it or render it anonymous. In practice:

  • Records relating to the exercise of your rights are kept for as long as necessary to demonstrate compliance, and then deleted.
  • Data we must retain by law, or data needed for actual or reasonably anticipated legal proceedings is kept for the applicable statutory or limitation period, after which it is deleted.

Retention periods may be adjusted where the law requires it, and where a specific incident makes longer retention necessary. Backup copies may persist for a limited additional period before being overwritten in the normal course of our service providers' backup rotation.

9. How we protect personal data

We apply appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data against accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure or access. These measures include:

  • encryption of data in transit between your browser and our website using HTTPS/TLS;
  • a deliberately minimal design: because this website has no accounts, no forms and no user database, most of the personal data that might otherwise be at risk is never collected or stored in the first place;
  • restricting access to data and to administrative interfaces to those who need it, on the basis of role and least privilege, and protecting that access with authentication controls;
  • reliance on established hosting and infrastructure providers that maintain their own security certifications, monitoring and incident-response processes, under contractual security and confidentiality obligations;
  • logging, monitoring and prompt handling of security events, and periodic review of our practices and of this Policy.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. If a personal data breach occurs that is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will notify the competent supervisory authority and, where the law requires it, you, without undue delay.

12. Your rights

Subject to the conditions and exceptions set out in the applicable law, you have the following rights in relation to the personal data we process about you:

  1. Right of access — to obtain confirmation of whether we process personal data about you, and to receive a copy of that data together with the information required by law (the purposes of processing, the categories of data, the recipients, the intended retention period, the source of the data, and information about your rights and about transfers to third countries).
  2. Right to rectification — to have inaccurate or incomplete personal data corrected.
  3. Right to erasure (the “right to be forgotten”) — to have personal data deleted where, for example, it is no longer necessary for the purposes for which it was collected, you have withdrawn your consent and there is no other legal basis, you have successfully objected to the processing, or the data has been processed unlawfully.
  4. Right to restriction of processing — to have us limit the use of personal data, for example while the accuracy of the data is being verified or while we assess your objection.
  5. Right to data portability — to receive the personal data you provided to us in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format, and to have it transmitted to another controller, where the processing is based on your consent or on a contract and is carried out by automated means.
  6. Right to object — to object, at any time and on grounds relating to your particular situation, to processing based on our legitimate interests (including the processing described in section 4). We will then stop processing that data unless we demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or unless the processing is necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.
  7. Right to withdraw consent — where processing is based on your consent, to withdraw it at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of processing carried out before the withdrawal.
  8. Rights relating to automated decisions — not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that produces legal effects concerning you or similarly significantly affects you. As explained in section 10, we do not carry out such processing.

The Law of Georgia on Personal Data Protection also gives you rights of information, access, rectification, erasure or blocking of data, objection to processing, and appeal to the supervisory authority or to a court, on substantially similar terms. Where the two regimes differ, we apply the standard that gives you the higher level of protection in the circumstances.

How to exercise your rights

Send your request to info@worldunion.live or by post to the address in section 1, marked “Privacy Request”. Please describe what you want us to do and give us enough information to identify you and to find the data concerned — for example, the email address you used to contact us, or the dates of your correspondence.

Exercising your rights is free of charge in ordinary circumstances. We will respond within the time required by applicable law. Under Georgian law, many data-subject requests must be handled within 10 working days. Where permitted by law, this period may be extended in specific circumstances. Where the GDPR applies, the general response period is one month, subject to the extensions permitted by the GDPR. If we cannot act on your request, we will explain the reasons and tell you about your right to complain and to seek a judicial remedy.

If we have reasonable doubts about your identity, we may ask you for additional information needed to confirm it. We ask for this only to make sure that we do not disclose personal data to the wrong person, and we will not use it for other purposes.

  • If you are in Georgia, you may lodge a complaint with the State Audit Office of Georgia, which is the competent supervisory authority for personal data protection. You may also apply to a court in accordance with Georgian law.

You can find the competent authority at the European Data Protection Board's website, or at the Information Commissioner's Office for the United Kingdom.
